My dissertation develops statistical methods for rare-variant association tests and variant prioritization in biobank-scale data. I also work on causal inference for time-to-event outcomes.

### Rare-variant association testing and variant interpretation

- **VACANT** uses continuous functional annotation scores to stratify rare variants, tests association adaptively, and estimates effect sizes for each annotation stratum. Platform talk at IGES 2026; posters at ASHG 2024, ASHG 2025, and the 2025 Taipei International Statistical Symposium and ICSA International Conference.
- **VACANT-M** is a mixed-model extension of VACANT that accounts for genetic relatedness and population structure in biobank cohorts (poster at IGES 2025).
- **SHARC** improves severity prediction for loss-of-function SNVs and indels and recalibrates coding-variant annotation scores across variant types for clinical variant classification (poster at ASHG 2026).

### Causal inference for time-to-event outcomes

- **Mediation with causally ordered mediators under the Cox model** ([*Statistics in Medicine*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8058)). Existing multi-mediator methods for survival outcomes relied on special model forms, such as probit or additive hazards models, or on a rare-outcome assumption. We defined path-specific effects through causally ordered mediators under the Cox proportional hazards model without the rare-outcome assumption, derived closed-form expressions on a transformed survival time and on survival probabilities, and applied the method to a hepatitis study of liver cancer risk.
- **Semi-competing risks.** We extended this framework to semi-competing risks with a semiparametric approach that gives inference on pathway-specific cumulative hazards (presented at the 28th South Taiwan Statistics Conference, 2019).
- **Information borrowing for survival prediction.** An adaptive information-borrowing framework for the Cox model that quantifies heterogeneity between a target triple-negative breast cancer dataset and a larger cohort, to calibrate how much information to share.

### Collaborative genetic studies

- **Genetics of stuttering** ([*Nature Genetics*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02267-2)). Sex- and ancestry-stratified genome-wide association analyses and meta-analyses of more than one million individuals identified 57 loci. I contributed a secondary heterogeneity analysis.
